**AMMMI (Aung Myint Moh Min Insurance Company Limited / အောင်မြင့်မိုရ်မင်း အာမခံ ကုမ္ပဏီ လီမိတက်) ownership trail — system-first, evidence-based summary**

### Confirmed registration and basic facts
- **Company name**: Aung Myint Moh Min Insurance Company Limited (also rendered Aung Myint Mo Min / AMMMI).
- **Registration**: Private Company Limited by Shares. DICA registration number **175848193**. Incorporation date listed as 2 November 2012 (or around that period); life insurance licence granted by the Insurance Supervisory Board on 25 May 2013; operations started late June 2013.
- **Business**: Life insurance only (initially). Address historically listed as No. 28, corner of Shwedagon Pagoda Road and Pantra Street, Dagon Township, Yangon.
- **Status**: Still registered on Myanmar Companies Online (MyCO) as of the latest accessible data. Free public profile shows basic details only; full shareholder lists, directors, and annual returns require paid extracts or restricted access under current conditions.

### Ownership and control layers (what open sources establish)

**1. Legal / formal ownership**  
Publicly available MyCO free view does **not** disclose the current shareholder register or beneficial owners. Myanmar corporate records for military-linked entities are opaque. No complete, verified, up-to-date list of legal owners or percentage holdings is freely published.

**2. Significant personal stake**  
Multiple independent sources (UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Myanmar 2019 report, RFA, Irrawaddy, Wikipedia summaries of open reporting) state that **Aung Pyae Sone** (son of Senior General Min Aung Hlaing) holds a “significant stake” in AMMMI. He is repeatedly described as owning or controlling the company among his portfolio (alongside Sky One Construction and other interests). U.S. sanctions on Aung Pyae Sone in March 2021 explicitly referenced profits from businesses including this insurance company.

**3. Military conglomerate links (MEC / MEHL affiliation)**  
- Academic theses and reporting describe AMMMI as a “semi-government” company established “with the full support of Myanma Economic Corporation (MEC)” and operating under the direction of the Ministry of Planning and Finance, the Insurance Supervisory Board, the chairman of MEC, and the company’s own board.
- The 2019 UN Fact-Finding Mission listed “Aung Myint Moh Min Insurance Corporation” among alleged subsidiaries or closely affiliated entities of the military conglomerates MEHL and MEC.
- RFA and other outlets describe it as “believed to be a subsidiary of Myanmar Economic Corporation.” Exact equity percentage or formal subsidiary status is not confirmed in open filings.

**MEC** is controlled by the Ministry of Defence / Quartermaster General’s Office and is a direct revenue source for the Tatmadaw. **MEHL** is owned by active/retired military personnel (individual and unit/institutional shareholders) with senior commanders (including Min Aung Hlaing as Patron Group chairman) exercising high-level influence. Both conglomerates are under international sanctions.

**4. Operational control and origin story**  
Reporting indicates that Lieutenant-General Min Naing (then Quartermaster-General) facilitated the partnership that led to the creation of AMMMI and the subsequent requirement that Tatmadaw personnel take life policies with the company. This shifted the military life-insurance book away from the state-owned Myanma Insurance.

### What remains unknown / unverified (honest gaps)
- Exact current shareholding percentages (legal owners vs beneficial owners).
- Full list of directors and controllers on the latest annual return.
- Precise equity relationship with MEC or MEHL (subsidiary, affiliate, or merely “supported by”).
- Any changes in ownership structure after 2021 sanctions or post-coup.
- Ultimate economic beneficiaries beyond the widely reported stake of Aung Pyae Sone and the military conglomerate influence.
- Internal board composition and decision-making authority over claims.

Myanmar’s corporate disclosure environment, especially for military-linked entities, does not provide transparent, audited beneficial-ownership data comparable to open jurisdictions. Paid DICA extracts or leaked filings would be required for higher resolution; those are not freely available in open sources.

### Sentinel assessment language
- **Confirmed**: Company exists, registered as private limited company, holds life licence since 2013, military personnel directed to it, significant stake held by Aung Pyae Sone, strong operational and founding links to MEC.
- **Supported / Plausible**: Treated as military-conglomerate affiliated or de-facto controlled for the Tatmadaw life book.
- **Unknown**: Precise percentage ownership, full beneficial-ownership chain, current director list, exact legal instruments transferring the military portfolio.

**Doctrine reminder** (Classroom 36): Legal owner ≠ shareholder list ≠ director/controller ≠ beneficial owner ≠ economic beneficiary. Trace the system (registration → licence → control structures → money flows) before naming individuals. Public records first; do not invent the missing percentages.

Sources for further chase: MyCO paid extracts (if accessible), 2019 UN FFM economic interests report and annexes, academic theses referencing AMMMI annual reports (2018–19 period), contemporaneous RFA/Irrawaddy reporting with named defectors, and any future DICA or regulatory filings. Always cross-check and apply falsification tests.

### Confirmed facts from open sources (as of available reporting up to early 2025–2026)

Under the Myanma Insurance Law 1993 (Chapter VI, Section 13), life insurance for military personnel (officers and other ranks aged 18–55) was compulsory through the state-owned Myanma Insurance. Maximum cover was set (historically up to 30 million kyats in older references), with premiums calculated from age and pay scale; medical exams required above certain sums.

After the private sector opened (around 2012–2013), Aung Myint Moh Min Insurance (AMMMI / Aung Myint Mo Min) received a life licence. Multiple independent reports (RFA, Irrawaddy, UN references, academic theses) state that by ~2015 military personnel were required or directed to take policies with AMMMI, displacing or largely replacing the prior Myanma Insurance military product for Tatmadaw members. Premiums are deducted from salary. Lowest-rank policies have been described as minimum two-year cover around 500,000 kyats with monthly premiums in the 8,000+ kyat range (figures vary by rank and year; one captain’s 2020 payslip showed a 25,000 kyat deduction).

Ownership links: Aung Pyae Sone (son of Senior General Min Aung Hlaing) has held a significant stake; the company is described as connected to military economic structures (MEHL/MEC references appear in reporting). U.S. sanctions targeted Aung Pyae Sone in 2021 partly on these grounds. An academic thesis on customer perception of AMMMI noted that military life insurance was its largest earner and that claim payouts in one earlier period (2018–19) were under 7% of premiums collected.

Post-2021 reporting from defectors, families, and outlets consistently describes difficulties obtaining payouts: bodies unrecovered → often listed “missing” rather than dead; paperwork held at unit level; families unaware of exact policy details or amounts; commanders alleged to skim; claims time-limited or rejected on technicalities; some defected personnel lose coverage. Aggregate official statistics on claims filed / pending / paid / rejected are not publicly released in usable form.

These are corroborated journalistic and secondary accounts. They are not the same as audited corporate filings, complete policy wordings, or court-verified ledgers.

### What remains unknown or unverified (honest gaps)

- Exact legal instrument (specific directive, order, or contract) that made AMMMI the exclusive or compulsory vehicle for Tatmadaw life cover after 2015. Public company registration and full shareholder registers are opaque under current conditions.
- Precise beneficial ownership chain beyond the widely reported stake held by Aung Pyae Sone and any MEHL/MEC interest. Legal owner ≠ shareholder list ≠ director/controller ≠ ultimate economic beneficiary.
- Full policy wording on “missing” vs declared dead, waiting periods, proof requirements, and when a claim can be paid without a recovered body.
- Current aggregate claim data: number of active policies, premiums collected annually, claims filed, rejection rates and stated reasons, payout totals, reserve levels, independent audits.
- Exact payroll deduction mechanism and accounting trail (salary → deduction → premium account → reserve → payout).
- Regulatory actions by the Insurance Business Supervisory Board (or successor) regarding this book of business since 2021.
- Scale of non-payment after heavy casualties; how many families never file because of lack of notification or documents.

Money does not vanish in a closed accounting system (inflow − outflow = balance change). Without ledgers we cannot quantify leakage, retention, or under-reservation. Political-economy questions (who bears the risk, who receives the premium, who decides claims, who ultimately benefits) remain open until documents appear.

### Star questions for systemic scrutiny (parliament-style or research-grade)

These stay institutional. They do not name private individuals as guilty; they demand documents and data. In the current Myanmar political environment any formal parliamentary questioning carries severe risk; the value lies in the questions themselves as accountability instruments and research templates for open-source work.

1. Under which specific law, regulation, directive or contract is life insurance participation for Tatmadaw personnel compulsory, and which entity is the designated insurer?
2. Is individual written consent required, or does an organisational order automatically enrol personnel and authorise salary deduction?
3. What is the legal basis, accounting procedure and banking path for premium deductions from military salaries?
4. What is the complete legal ownership, shareholding, directorate and beneficial-ownership structure of the insurer currently handling the military book?
5. How does the policy define and treat “missing” status versus declared death when a body is not recovered? What evidence and waiting period trigger a claim decision?
6. For personnel declared missing or killed without recovered remains, what is the exact procedure, timeline and decision authority for claim payment or rejection?
7. What aggregate statistics exist (and can be released) on active policies, premiums collected, claims filed, approved, pending, rejected, and paid for the military life book since 2015 / since 2021?
8. Which regulatory authority supervises this portfolio, what audits or regulatory reports exist, and what appeal or review mechanism is available to beneficiaries when a claim is denied?

Additional deeper probes (ownership separation, conflict-of-interest between employer / insurer / regulator, transparency of claim ledger architecture) follow the same pattern.

### Practical Sentinel method for journalists and researchers

- Start with the claim → extract testable propositions → convert each into a research question that points to a public or lawfully obtainable record.
- Build a simple claim ledger (policy status, incident type, claim filed Y/N, status, decision date, reason, outcome) using aggregate or anonymised data where possible. Protect privacy; do not dox or harass.
- Prefer public sources: company filings where accessible, published regulations, Myanma Insurance historical materials, academic theses, credible journalism with named sources or documents, court records if lawfully available.
- Always ask the falsification question: “What evidence would force me to abandon or revise this hypothesis?”
- Confidence language is non-negotiable. “We do not yet know” is a professional statement, not a weakness.
- Safety doctrine: no private-database intrusion, no unauthorised access, no dissemination of personal data of non-public figures, no harassment. Civil intelligence ≠ surveillance.

**Final Classroom 36 teaching**  
A well-constructed research question is often more valuable than a premature answer because it maps the documents, data, institutions and decision-makers that must be examined. Raw intelligence becomes useful only after it is converted into questions that can be answered (or shown to be unanswerable) with evidence.  

The highest professional standard remains:  
State what is confirmed.  
State what is reported but unverified.  
State what is still unknown.  
Refuse to invent the missing pieces.

That is realism. That is the doctrine. Use the star questions as templates; chase the public records; protect sources and yourselves.
