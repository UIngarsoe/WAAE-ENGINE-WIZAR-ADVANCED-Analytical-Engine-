The February 2021 military coup had been followed by approximately **five years and six months** by 24 August 2026.

# 2. Methodological Rule — Claim ≠ Fact ≠ Assessment

Classroom 46 introduces a strict separation between three analytical layers.

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | What an actor says or officially declares |
| **Evidence** | Information that can be independently examined |
| **Assessment** | A reasoned interpretation based on available evidence |

A fourth category must also remain visible:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Uncertainty** | Information that cannot yet be established with sufficient confidence |

Therefore:

```text
CLAIM
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
VERIFICATION
  ↓
ANALYSIS
  ↓
ASSESSMENT
  ↓
UNCERTAINTY

This prevents SSISM intelligence education from becoming political storytelling.

4. Political Transformation

From Military Rule Toward a Claimed Political Transition

The post-coup period included attempts to construct a political transition through elections and institutional restructuring.

The central intelligence question is not simply:

> “Was an election held?”



The deeper questions are:

1. Who was permitted to participate?


2. Which political organizations were excluded?


3. Were major opposition figures free to participate?


4. Was political competition genuinely open?


5. Could voters make meaningful choices?


6. Did the resulting institutions possess independent authority?


7. Did the process produce broad domestic and international legitimacy?



Sentinel Assessment

An electoral event by itself does not establish democratic legitimacy.

Democratic legitimacy depends upon the surrounding conditions:

Election
+
Political Inclusion
+
Freedom of Participation
+
Institutional Independence
+
Meaningful Competition
+
Public Consent
=
Democratic Legitimacy

Therefore, Classroom 46 treats the political transition as an object of investigation, rather than automatically accepting either the official narrative or its opposite.

5. Humanitarian and Human Rights Reality

The post-coup period has been characterized by severe armed conflict, civilian displacement, political repression and allegations of serious violations of international human rights and humanitarian law.

The intelligence task is to separate:

verified incidents,

reported incidents,

allegations,

attribution,

legal interpretation,

and unresolved cases.


Analytical Rule

> A serious allegation requires serious evidence.



The same standard must apply regardless of which actor is accused.

The Sentinel therefore asks:

What happened?

Where did it happen?

When did it happen?

Who reported it?

What independent evidence exists?

Can the event be corroborated?

Who is alleged to be responsible?

What remains uncertain?

6. Civilian Protection as a Security Indicator

A state cannot be evaluated only through its ability to command armed forces.

A broader security audit must examine whether civilians can:

live safely,

travel,

work,

obtain food,

access healthcare,

educate their children,

maintain property,

and participate in public life.


Therefore:

> Civilian security is itself a national-security metric.



Military capability without civilian security represents only one dimension of state power.

10. The Five Core Audit Vectors

Classroom 46 therefore evaluates five major dimensions.

Vector	Core Question

1. Legitimacy	Does political authority possess meaningful public and institutional legitimacy?
2. Security	Has the state increased or decreased civilian and national security?
3. Governance	Has institutional capacity strengthened or deteriorated?
4. Economy	Has economic policy improved practical living conditions?
5. Human Rights	Has the protection of fundamental rights strengthened or deteriorated?


These variables should not be converted into a single numerical score unless the methodology, datasets and weighting system are independently disclosed.

16. Appamāda — အပ္ပမာဒ

Appamāda means heedfulness, vigilance and carefulness.

For intelligence education:

> Never become so confident that verification stops.



Before publishing an important assessment:

check the date,

check the source,

check the attribution,

check the quotation,

check the calculation,

check the chronology,

check alternative explanations,

and identify uncertainty.


This is the intellectual equivalent of a safety brake.

17. The Reality Before Narrative Protocol

Classroom 46 formalizes the following protocol:

REALITY BEFORE NARRATIVE

1. What happened?
2. What evidence exists?
3. Who independently reported it?
4. Can it be corroborated?
5. What is still uncertain?
6. What alternative explanation exists?
7. What conclusion is justified by the evidence?
8. What conclusion would be unjustified?

The final question is particularly important.

A strong analyst knows not only what can be said.

A strong analyst also knows:

> What cannot yet be said.
