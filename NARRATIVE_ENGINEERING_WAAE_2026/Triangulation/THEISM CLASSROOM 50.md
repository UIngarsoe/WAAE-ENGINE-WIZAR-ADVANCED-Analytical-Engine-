## 1. What Is Triangulation?

In the simplest terms: do not finish the analysis when one source states a claim. Cross-check the claim through separate, independent evidentiary pathways.

**Example**

Someone states: “Business conditions in this building are excellent.”

A Sentinel Analyst then looks for:

| Source | Content |
|--------|---------|
| **Source 1** — Management statement | “Business conditions are good.” |
| **Source 2** — Customer observation | “Customer complaints have declined.” |
| **Source 3** — Operational data | “Processing time has decreased.” |
| **Source 4** — Independent inspection | “Service quality has improved.” |

If all four sources merely reprint the same management press release, they are **not** four independent sources. They may be one piece of evidence restated four times.

> **This distinction is the heart of triangulation.**

---

## 2. “More Sources” Is Not the Same as Triangulation

Today’s most important lesson:

> **More sources ≠ More independent evidence**

If four outlets all restate the same official meeting statement, the correct description is not “four sources confirm it.”

The accurate description is:

> “Four outlets report the same underlying official claim.”

The Sentinel therefore asks not only:

- “How many sources?”

But:

- **“How many independent evidentiary pathways?”**

---

## 3. Basic Model of Triangulation

```
A — Source / Claim
      ↓  What is being stated?
B — Independent Corroboration
      ↓  What do other pathways say?
C — Contradiction / Alternative
      ↓  What evidence differs or challenges?
→ Assessment
      How much confidence is justified?
```

---

## 4. Source Independence Is Decisive

Suppose:

- **Source A** = government statement  
- **Source B** = newspaper article that only quotes Source A  

Then A and B are not fully independent.

```
A + B ≠ two independent confirmations
```

**Sentinel Question**

> “If Source A disappeared, would Source B still contain independent evidence?”

If the answer is no, dependency exists.

---

## 5. Four Types of Triangulation

### ① Source Triangulation

Compare different kinds of sources:

- Official source  
- Independent journalism  
- Academic research  
- Local reporting  

### ② Method Triangulation

Compare different methods of obtaining data:

- Interview  
- Statistics  
- Satellite imagery  
- Document analysis  
- Direct observation  

### ③ Temporal Triangulation

Compare different time periods (January → April → July → August).  
Do not rely on a single-day snapshot; examine the **trend**.

### ④ Perspective Triangulation

Include different actors’ viewpoints:

- Government  
- Citizens  
- Businesses  
- Opposition  
- International organisations  
- Researchers

## 7. Triangulation Matrix for a Positive Narrative Claim

**Claim under test:** “Government has improved stability and development.”

| Evidence Channel | Question to Ask |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Official statement | What exactly does the government claim? |
| Independent reporting | What do external sources observe? |
| Quantitative data | What do measurable indicators show? |
| Local / ground evidence | What do on-the-ground experiences indicate? |
| Time series | Is the situation actually improving over time? |
| Contradictory evidence | What challenges the claim? |
| Missing evidence | What cannot yet be checked? |

Then write the **Assessment**.

---

## 8. Triangulation ≠ “Majority Equals Truth”

If ten sources say “A is true” and one source says “A is not true,” do not automatically accept A because of the 10-to-1 ratio.

What matters is:

- Who knows what?  
- How did they know it?  
- Are their sources independent?  
- What evidence do they actually possess?  

---

## 9. Evidence Weight Is Not Equal

Not every piece of evidence carries the same weight.

| Type of Evidence | Relative Strength (heuristic) |
|------------------|-------------------------------|
| Single social-media comment | Weak |
| Manager / interested-party statement | Useful primary claim, interested source |
| Customer survey | Stronger |
| Independent audit | Stronger |
| Repeated operational metrics over time | Potentially strong |
| Independent methods converging on same finding | **Strongest form of corroboration** |

Context still matters. Do not treat any source type as permanently “strong” without examination.

---

## 10. What Is Corroboration?

Within triangulation, **corroboration** means that one piece of evidence can independently support another.

**Example**

- Satellite imagery shows increased construction activity  
- Company report states that the project expanded  
- Independent procurement records show increased material purchases  

These are different evidentiary pathways and therefore more meaningful.

---

## 11. Do Not Erase Contradiction

When evidence conflicts, the advanced analyst does not immediately delete one side.

First ask:

> “Why do they differ?”

Possible reasons:

- Time difference  
- Geographic difference  
- Measurement difference  
- Sampling difference  
- Source bias  
- Genuine disagreement  
- Changing situation  

**Contradiction is not always noise.**  
Sometimes contradiction itself is intelligence.

---

## 12. Contradiction Map

| Claim | Supporting | Contradicting | Unknown |
|-------|------------|---------------|---------|
| Stability improved | A, B | C, D | E |
| Economy improving | A | B, C | D |
| Legal order strengthened | A | C | B |
| Public trust increased | A | B, C | D |

Then ask:

- Which claim is strongest?  
- Which is weakest?  
- Which remains undecided?  

This is where analytic skill becomes visible.

---

## 13. Triangulation Protocol Against Confirmation Bias

The human mind notices supporting evidence more easily than challenging evidence.

**Sentinel Protocol**

1. Find supporting evidence  
2. Find contradictory evidence  
3. Find independent evidence  
4. Identify missing evidence  
5. Recalculate confidence  

This links Classroom 49’s hypothesis testing with Classroom 50’s triangulation.

Richards J. Heuer’s Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) framework likewise stresses testing alternatives and searching for **disconfirming evidence** rather than only supporting a preferred hypothesis.

---

## 14. Do Not Blindly Apply a “Three Sources Rule”

“At least three sources” should not be treated as an absolute rule.

- Sometimes a single original document is primary evidence  
- Ten sources may simply copy one another  

What matters is:

```
Independence + Reliability + Relevance + Diagnostic Value
```

---

## 15. Sentinel Evidence-Value Heuristic

```
Evidence Value ≈ Reliability × Independence × Relevance × Diagnostic Value
```

This is a **teaching heuristic**, not a mathematical law.

- A reliable but irrelevant source → low overall value  
- A highly relevant source that only reprints another source → low independence → low corroboration value  

---

## 16. Trace the Underlying Source

Two websites may look different yet rest on:

- The same press release  
- The same spokesperson  
- The same government document  
- The same wire report  
- The same unnamed source  

**Apparent diversity is not actual diversity.**

**Sentinel Question**

> “What is the underlying source?”

---

## 17. Source Dependency Chain

```
Government statement
       ↓
State newspaper
       ↓
International aggregator
       ↓
Social-media post
       ↓
AI summary
```

These five items are **not** five pieces of evidence.  
They may be a single information lineage.

The analyst must **trace backward to the originating evidence**.

---

## 18. Triangulation Matters Even More in the Age of AI

When an AI system says “according to multiple reports…,” the Sentinel does not accept it at face value.

Ask:

- Which reports?  
- Are they independent?  
- Do they share the same origin?  
- What is primary evidence?  
- What remains uncertain?  

```
AI-generated “multiple sources”
        ≠
Independently triangulated evidence
```

## 20. The “Three Windows” Exercise

Take a claim such as: “Institutional stability has improved.”

View it through three windows:

| Window | Question |
|--------|----------|
| 🪟 **1 — Official View** | What do they say? |
| 🪟 **2 — Independent View** | What does other evidence say? |
| 🪟 **3 — Contradictory View** | What challenges the claim? |

Then ask:

> “Do all three windows show the same scene?”

If not:

> “Why do they differ?”

## 22. Professional Language

Avoid easy certainty:

> “This proves…”

Prefer calibrated language:

- “This is consistent with…”  
- “This provides independent support for…”  
- “The evidence is mixed…”  
- “The claim remains insufficiently corroborated…”  
- “Available evidence does not yet distinguish between H1 and H2…”  
- “Confidence is moderate because…”  

This language calibrates certainty to evidence.

---

## 23. Highest Lesson of Classroom 50

The purpose of triangulation is **not** to collect the largest number of sources.

The purpose is to reach a **better-justified judgment**.

Therefore:

> Three genuinely independent, relevant evidence streams  
> can be more valuable than  
> one hundred weak or dependent sources.

```
TRIANGULATE → CORROBORATE → CALIBRATE → UPDATE
```

**Source · Method · Time · Perspective**

1. See one point → do not yet believe  
2. Hear one source → do not yet conclude  
3. Trace the origin  
4. Test independence  
5. Seek support  
6. Seek contradiction  
7. Separate the unknown

10. Update when new evidence arrives
